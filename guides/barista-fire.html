Part-time income

Barista FIRE: what does part-time pay actually cover?

Barista FIRE means the portfolio does not pay the whole budget. A part-time job covers the rest. Without that income, this example keeps a balance on 4 of 6 historical windows and 520 of 1,000 shuffled paths. With $1,500 a month for 15 years, the same plan keeps a balance on 6 of 6 windows and 745 of 1,000 shuffled paths.

Methodology

The usual barista sketch is a portfolio that is too small for the full budget, plus a job with benefits or a paycheck that covers the gap. FIREcalc has no barista switch. The retirement calculator already has “other income,” with a start that is immediate and a duration you type. That is the field this page uses.

One portfolio, two runs

Age 45, $700,000, spending $48,000, raised with inflation. Stocks 60%, tax 0%, horizon 45 years, Social Security off. $48,000 is 6.9% of $700,000. 4 of 6 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. The 1976 window ran out in year 35, at age 80. The 1977 window ran out in year 28, at age 73. 520 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon.

The second run adds $1,500 a month, $18,000 a year, for 15 years (age 45 through 59). The first-year portfolio draw falls to $30,000, which is 4.3% of $700,000. 6 of 6 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. 745 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. The job did not make the shuffled paths safe. It moved the count from 520 of 1,000 to 745 of 1,000.

What this model leaves out

  • Inflation on the paycheck. The $18,000 never rises. The spending number does. Each year the job covers less of the budget, and at age 60 it covers nothing.
  • Health insurance from the job. If the job is how you get coverage, losing it means the spending number has to rise. Put that premium in spending. The engine will not price it.
  • A tax on the paycheck. The tax rate here is 0%. A simple tax rate grosses the spending figure up before other income is subtracted.

Type the pay you would actually earn, and the number of years you would actually work. A coast check, with savings at zero and no withdrawals yet, is a different question. It is on the Coast FIRE guide.

Questions people ask next

Where do I enter the part-time job?

Under Social Security and other income, as other monthly income, with a duration. Leave the spending field as the full budget. Subtracting the job from spending and also entering it as income counts it twice.

Does the side income rise with inflation?

No. Other income and a pension stay flat in nominal dollars. Spending rises with CPI when that box is on. A job whose pay rises would be a larger number than this example uses.

Why did two historical windows fail without the job?

The 1976 and 1977 windows ran out of money. $48,000 is 6.9% of $700,000. The part-time pay cuts the first-year portfolio draw to $30,000.

Is Coast FIRE the same thing?

No. Coast FIRE, on this site, is a savings question with new contributions at zero. Barista FIRE is a retirement question: you are already spending, and a job covers part of it. The coast guide shows the zero-contribution check.
