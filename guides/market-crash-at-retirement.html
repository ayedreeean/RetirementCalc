Bad first years

What if the market crashes the year I retire?

A crash in the first year hurts because you withdraw while prices are down. On a 60/40 mix in this table, 2008 was a −14.16% portfolio year and 2022 was −17.96%. The year 2000 was not: Treasuries rose enough that the same mix was up 1.20%, and the damage came in the years after. None of the six named stress packs ran out of money on the $1.2 million, $48,000 plan below. Several of them are shorter than 30 years, so “did not run out” is not a 30-year verdict.

Methodology

The year you retire gets more attention than it deserves as a single number, and less attention than it deserves as a sequence. One down year does not decide a 30-year retirement. It does force a withdrawal at bad prices, and the following years decide whether that bite heals. This page replays the bad stretches the file actually contains.

The plan the packs use

Age 55, $1,200,000, spending $48,000, which is 4%. Stocks 60%, 10-year Treasuries 40%, tax 0%, inflation on, Social Security off, horizon 30 years when the pack is long enough. 21 of 21 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. 985 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon.

In 2008 the table’s stocks were −37.00% and Treasuries were +20.10%. The 60/40 mix was −14.16%. In 2022 stocks were −18.04% and Treasuries were −17.83%, so the mix was −17.96%. Bonds did not cushion that year. In 2000 stocks were −9.10% and Treasuries were +16.66%, so the mix was +1.20%. Calling 2000 a first-year crash is wrong for this mix. Calling the following decade fine is also wrong, which is why the pack exists.

The six packs on this plan

  • Dot-com hangover, 2000–2024, 25 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 60% of the start in 2008, and the path finished at 77% of the starting balance, smaller in today’s dollars. Open it.
  • GFC crash, 2008–2024, 17 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 79% of the start in 2008, and the path finished at 134% of the starting balance, ahead in today’s dollars. Open it.
  • 2022 rate shock, 2022–2024, 3 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 72% of the start in 2022, and the path finished at 83% of the starting balance, smaller in today’s dollars. Open it.
  • Stagflation squeeze, 1977–2006, 30 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 64% of the start in 1981, and the path finished at 252% of the starting balance, ahead in today’s dollars. Open it.
  • Rate wall of ’81, 1981–2010, 30 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 87% of the start in 1981, and the path finished at 461% of the starting balance, ahead in today’s dollars. Open it.
  • Bond rout of ’94, 1994–2023, 30 years. The path survived. Purchasing power fell to 91% of the start in 1994, and the path finished at 262% of the starting balance, ahead in today’s dollars. Open it.

A challenge link such as /?pack=gfc names the pack only. The browser then uses whatever plan is already saved on that device. The buttons above pin this page’s inputs so the path is the one described here. The sequence page shows the same idea across every complete 40-year window, not only the named villains.

Surviving a pack that ends in 2024 is not evidence about a retirement that still has years to go. The dot-com pack is 25 years. The GFC pack is 17. The 2022 pack is 3. This is a planning sketch, not advice.

Questions people ask next

Did the 2008 path run out of money?

No, inside the years the file has. The GFC pack is 2008–2024, 17 years. The balance fell to 79% of the start in 2008 and finished at 134% of the start. Seventeen years is not a full retirement.

Was 2000 a crash for a 60/40 portfolio?

Not in year one. Stocks were −9.10% and 10-year Treasuries were +16.66%, so the mix was +1.20%. The pack still finished smaller than it started, at 77% of the start, with a low of 60% in 2008.

Why was 2022 different?

Stocks and Treasuries fell together: −18.04% and −17.83%. The 60/40 mix was −17.96%. The pack is only three years long, 2022–2024.

What about a full 30-year success rate?

21 of 21 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. 985 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. The packs are single paths. The success counts are the other statistic. Neither one includes 1966 or 1973–74.
