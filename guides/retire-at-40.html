Age 40

How much do I need to retire at 40?

For $62,000 a year, a 4% sketch is $1,550,000. That is 25 times spending. The hard part at 40 is the length: a retirement to age 90 is 50 years, and this table has 1 complete 50-year window. 1 of 1 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. That single window is 1975–2024. Shuffled years, which redraw the same 50 rows, kept a balance on 951 of 1,000 paths.

Methodology

Retiring at 40 is two questions. The savings question is whether the portfolio reaches a number while you still have a paycheck. The withdrawal question is whether that number can pay your bills for about half a century. A 30-year 4% study does not answer the second one.

The number, then the 50-year test

$62,000 × 25 = $1,550,000. Stocks on the withdrawal are 75%, tax is 0%, spending rises with inflation, Social Security is off. 1 of 1 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. Read that as “the only complete window in the file survived,” not as a success rate. 951 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. The earliest shuffled failure ran out in year 15.

At 3.5%, the portfolio for the same $62,000 is $1,771,429. 970 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. Moving from 4% to 3.5% meant saving more before 40. It did not change the fact that the historical file contains one 50-year span.

A savings path that is aiming at 40

Age 30, $180,000 invested, $125,000 of income, $62,000 of spending, so $63,000 a year is saved. Real raises are 2%. Stocks are 80%. The goal is the 4% portfolio, $1,550,000 in today’s dollars.

On seed 246813, the median path takes 11 years, age 41. The faster 10% are there by 9 years, age 39. The slower 10% take at least 14 years, age 44. 1,000 of 1,000 shuffled paths reached the goal inside 50 years. Age 41 is 1 year after 40 on this example. The faster 10% arrive at age 39. The slower 10% are age 44 or later. 40 of 50 start years reach the goal before the sample ends. The median of all 50 start years is 11 years. The faster 10% are there by 9 years. Starts near 2024 have only a few years of data, so the slower tail includes windows that run out of history. If the median is past the birthday you care about, the plan is the savings rate or the spending number, not a different label.

Compare this with retiring at 45 and retiring at 50, where the historical sample has more than one window. The withdrawal-rate page compares 4%, 3.5%, and 3.25% on a fixed $1 million.

Questions people ask next

Why not trust the one historical window?

It is a single path, 1975 through 2024. It survived on this plan. A success rate needs more than one cohort, and the hardest US start years are before this table.

Does the savings example arrive by 40?

On seed 246813, the median path takes 11 years, which is age 41. The faster 10% are there by 9 years. The slower 10% take at least 14 years.

What is the 3.5% portfolio for the same spending?

$62,000 divided by 0.035 is $1,771,429. The shuffled test of that larger portfolio is on the third button. Spending does not change.

Where is Social Security?

Off. Someone retiring at 40 may not claim for more than twenty years, and the benefit itself depends on the earnings record, which this tool does not compute. The Social Security guide shows a later example with the benefit turned on.
