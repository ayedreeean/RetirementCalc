The number

What is my FIRE number?

Your FIRE number is the portfolio that matches a spending level and a withdrawal rate. $48,000 divided by 0.04 is $1,200,000. $48,000 divided by 0.035 is $1,371,429. The 4% portfolio, tested for 40 years on this sample, survived on 11 of 11 historical windows and 963 of 1,000 shuffled paths. The number is the start of the test, not the result.

Methodology

The shortcut is famous because the arithmetic is short. Choose the annual spending you need in today’s dollars. Divide by the withdrawal rate you are willing to put through a long test. Four percent gives 25 times spending. Three and a half percent gives a larger pile. Then test the pile, because a multiple copied from a 30-year study is not yet your retirement.

Both tests use 60% stocks, 0% tax, inflation adjustments, Social Security off, and a 40-year horizon. A 40-year retirement has 11 complete windows in the 1975–2024 table.

How long the 4% number took to save

Age 36, $160,000 invested, $115,000 of income, $67,000 of spending. The gap is $48,000 a year, which happens to equal the retirement budget and is not the same thing as the withdrawal. Real raises are 2%. Stocks are 75%. The goal is $1,200,000 in today’s dollars.

On seed 246813, the median path takes 10 years, age 46. The faster 10% are there by 9 years, age 45. The slower 10% take at least 13 years, age 49. 1,000 of 1,000 shuffled paths reached the goal inside 50 years. 40 of 50 start years reach the goal before the sample ends. The median of all 50 start years is 11 years. The faster 10% are there by 9 years. Starts near 2024 have only a few years of data, so the slower tail includes windows that run out of history. Open the retirement link when you want the success count, not another year count. The calculator page starts from the same pair of examples.

Taxes, a pension, and Social Security all change the portfolio you need. Leave them off and the number is a portfolio-only sketch. Turn them on and say that you did. The when-can-I-retire guide is the same savings question with a different household.

Questions people ask next

Is the FIRE number always 25 times spending?

Only if you are testing 4%. Twenty-five times spending is 1 divided by 0.04. A 3.5% test is about 28.6 times spending. Pick the rate, then multiply.

How long did $1.2 million take in the example?

On seed 246813, the median path takes 10 years, age 46. The faster 10% are there by 9 years. The slower 10% take at least 13 years.

Does hitting the number mean I can retire?

It means the savings goal was reached. The withdrawal test is separate. On the 4% portfolio, 963 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon.

What about the larger 3.5% portfolio?

$1,371,429 with the same $48,000 of spending survived on 981 of 1,000 shuffled 40-year paths. You have to save the extra $171,429 first.
