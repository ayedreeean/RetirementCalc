Fat FIRE: does a high budget change the test?

Fat FIRE is a large annual spending number, so the portfolio has to be large too. $180,000 is 4% of $4,500,000. The percentage test is the same one a lean plan uses. On a 40-year horizon, 11 of 11 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. Methodology

Fat FIRE is the community name for retiring on a spending level that is high by ordinary standards: travel, a costly city, help around the house. None of that is a field in the calculator. You type the dollars. If those dollars are 4% of the portfolio, you are running a 4% test, whatever you call it.

Spending $180,000 for 40 years

The withdrawal link starts at age 51, with the $4,500,000 already in hand, and runs 40 years. Stocks 60%, Treasuries 40%, tax 0%, Social Security off, spending raised with inflation. A 40-year retirement has 11 complete windows in the 1975–2024 table. 11 of 11 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. 963 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. The earliest shuffled failure lasted 19 years. Eleven historical windows that all begin in 1975–1985 are not the late-1960s retirements in the published 4% studies. The 4% rule page says the same thing about the smaller textbook case.

A fat plan is fragile in the savings phase if the income that funds the $140,000 gap stops. It is fragile in retirement if spending rises above the figure you typed, or if taxes turn a 4% draw into something larger. Change one field on the open calculator and read the new count. This is an illustration, not advice.

Questions people ask next

Is fat FIRE a different formula?

No. It is a higher spending number divided by the withdrawal rate you want to test. Four percent of $4.5 million is $180,000. Four percent of $800,000 is $32,000. The engine treats them the same way.

Why can a high budget still show a high success count?

Because the portfolio is scaled to the spending. A 4% withdrawal on a large balance is still 4%. What changes is whether you can save the larger balance, and whether taxes make the draw bigger than 4%.

Does this include a 15% tax?

No. The tax rate is 0% so the portfolio draw stays at $180,000. A simple tax rate grosses spending up by dividing by 1 minus the rate. At 15% the draw would be larger than 4%.

How long did the savings example take?

On seed 246813, the median path takes 9 years. The faster 10% are there by 7 years. The slower 10% take at least 12 years.
