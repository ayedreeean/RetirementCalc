Order of returns

Sequence of returns risk: same plan, different start year

Sequence of returns risk is the damage from bad returns early, while you are withdrawing, even if the average return over the whole retirement would have been fine. On this $1 million, $40,000, 60/40 plan, all 11 complete 40-year windows survived. The 1977 window finished at $3,619,589 in today’s dollars. The 1982 window finished at $13,102,244.

Methodology

Two retirees can earn the same average return and end in different places if one of them gets the bad years first. Withdrawals turn a temporary loss into a permanent one, because you sell shares while prices are down and those shares are not there for the recovery. That is the sequence problem. A shuffled-year success rate, which draws each year independently, is a useful stress test and a bad picture of this particular risk.

Eleven real 40-year sequences

Portfolio $1,000,000. First-year spending $40,000, then raised with CPI. Stocks 60%, 10-year Treasuries 40%. Tax 0%. Social Security off. Horizon 40 years. 11 of 11 complete windows kept a balance above zero at the end of every year. The start years that fit are 1975 through 1985.

The 1977 window is the low ending: $3,619,589 in today’s dollars, after a trough of 64% of the starting balance in 1981. The 1982 window finished at $13,102,244, and its lowest point was 100% of the start. Both paid the same $40,000 bills. The order of the years did the rest. Ending balances are purchasing power, portfolio divided by cumulative CPI, not a nominal scoreboard.

963 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. Those paths are not the rows above. Each shuffled year is an independent draw, so a bad year is not followed by the year that actually followed it.

The named stress packs on the crash page are one path each, including stretches shorter than 40 years. They answer “what did this specific bad start do,” which is the sequence question in a single window.

Questions people ask next

Did anyone run out of money?

Not in these 11 historical windows. 963 of 1,000 shuffled paths kept a balance above zero for every year of the horizon. Survival is not the same as an ending balance. The endings on this page differ by millions of today’s dollars.

Why is the 1977 start the painful one here?

It is the complete 40-year window with the lowest ending balance in today’s dollars, $3,619,589, and its purchasing power fell to 64% of the start in 1981. The 1982 start never fell below the starting balance.

Is this the famous 1966 retiree?

No. The table starts in 1975. A 1966 or 1973–74 retirement is not a window here. The spread you see is inside the years the file actually has.

What should I do with the gap between endings?

Treat the worse window as information about order, not as a forecast of your retirement. Spending, the stock share, and a lower withdrawal rate move the whole set. Change one of them in the open calculator.
